# Site-Type Guide: Blog / Content Site

Pairs with `workflows/content-strategy.md`, `workflows/content-optimization.md`, and `workflows/keyword-research.md`. Covers structural notes specific to content-heavy sites.

## Typical stacks

Blog/content sites span the full range: a headless CMS (Contentful, Sanity, Strapi, WordPress-as-CMS) feeding a Next.js/Gatsby/Astro frontend, WordPress natively (see `frameworks/wordpress.md`), or a static-site generator reading Markdown (Eleventy, Hugo, Jekyll, Astro content collections — see `frameworks/static-html.md` for the general pattern). Identify the actual content source of truth during discovery — metadata/schema for Article/BlogPosting content should bind to that source's real fields (real publish date, real author, real category), not be hardcoded per template.

## Article/BlogPosting schema specifics

This is the site type where date and author accuracy in schema matters most and is checked most closely (see [[../rules/schema-rules]] and [[../rules/no-fabrication]]):

- `datePublished` — bind to the CMS/frontmatter's real publish date field, or git history/file creation as a fallback signal if no CMS field exists. Never today's date as a placeholder.
- `dateModified` — bind to a real last-edited signal; if the CMS doesn't track this distinctly from publish date, it's fine to omit rather than fabricate a value.
- `author` — real author name/bio if the site attributes authorship; if content is unattributed/team-authored, don't invent an individual author to satisfy a schema field — use `Organization` as author, or omit, rather than fabricate a person.

## Topical architecture

Content sites benefit most from deliberate hub/cluster structure (`workflows/information-architecture.md`, `workflows/internal-linking.md`) — pillar pages linking to and from cluster content on the same topic. Check whether this exists as an intentional pattern or whether content is flat/unstructured (a common finding worth flagging even outside a dedicated audit).

## Category/tag pages

Often auto-generated by the CMS/generator with thin or no unique content (just a list of post titles). Evaluate against [[../rules/content-quality]] — a tag page with zero unique content and only 1-2 posts is a thin-content candidate for `noindex` rather than an indexing target; a well-populated, well-curated category page with real descriptive content is a legitimate indexable asset.

## Content freshness

For evergreen topics, periodic content updates (refreshing stats, examples, screenshots) are a legitimate and often high-ROI `workflows/content-optimization.md` activity — but `dateModified` should only update when a genuine, meaningful edit occurred, not as a cosmetic "freshness" signal with no real change (this crosses into [[../rules/no-fabrication]] territory if done cosmetically).

## Never

Never fabricate author bios/credentials to simulate expertise (see [[../rules/content-quality]]'s E-E-A-T guidance). Never backdate or "freshen" a date without a real corresponding content change.
